Finally, we conclude by mentioning that this work is a first step in deriving an algorithm that possesses
accelerated local rates and takes into account that real-world problems may have Hessians that are
costly to evaluate (this is indeed the case in our neural network example of Section 5.1). There are, of
course, many additional aspects that one could do to increase the efficiency of the proposed method,
e.g., by employing such tools as sketching or other randomization techniques, quasi-Newton updates,
etc. We leave these interesting aspects for future work.
