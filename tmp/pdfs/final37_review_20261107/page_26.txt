The main technical difficulties that we resolve in this paper lie in the construction of the stopping
conditions that allow us to obtain all the results in a completely class- and parameter-agnostic way.
In particular, compared to other adaptive second-order methods, we had to introduce the second
inequality in line (2) of Algorithm 1, which is crucial for obtaining the convergence of the iterates
under PL condition. Another main technical challenge was to prove the superlinear and γ-order
superlinear convergence. The high-level idea is to show that the semismoothness allows us to obtain
an analogue of (37), but with a constant L which goes to 0 with a certain rate. The next step is to show
that locally the regularization parameter λk also goes to 0 with a certain rate, which eventually gives
the desired improved rates under semismoothness. In that way, we strictly prove that our stopping
conditions allow the algorithm to fully adapt to the local geometry of the objective function. To show
the improved local rate in Theorem 4.5, we had to introduce gradient regularization with a careful
choice of the exponent p and show that this does not ruin other results like global rates. Finally, we
prove all this despite the fact that the algorithm uses “old” Hessians from the previous iterations, and
the challenge was to show that this does not ruin other results, especially the local rates.
The introduction mentions Huber, squared hinge, and sparsemax losses. All these losses are convex,
have strongly-semismooth gradients, and Assumption 1 holds, thus, Theorem 3.5 holds. Moreover, if
one adds quadratic regularization, then these regularized losses become strongly convex, and many of
our theoretical results apply.
According to Theorem 4.4, the whole convergence picture is as follows. From any starting point, the
method converges linearly to a solution x∗ thanks to the PL condition. Since the linear convergence
xk →x ∗ is guaranteed, at some point xk starts to belong to the ball around x∗ where the local strong
convexity of Assumption 2 holds. The asymptotic semismoothness Assumption 3 and DM conditions
also start to work after some iteration when xk is sufficiently close to x∗. All this implies that λk →0
and the method starts to converge superlinearly when λk is sufficiently small. In that sense, the
superlinear convergence is local and asymptotic, but the linear rate is global and nonasymptotic.
Theorem 4.8 complements the convergence picture by quantifying the order of local superlinear
convergence underγ-order semismoothness in Assumption 4.
Compared to the existing literature on the global rates for second-order methods, which require
f∈C 2,2, our results are obtained under a weaker Assumption 1, which does not even require that
f∈C 2. For example, max{a⊤x−b,0} 2 satisfies our Assumptions 1, 3 and 4 while not being
twice continuously differentiable, nor having Lipschitz Hessian. Further, existing works obtain local
superlinear convergence under Lipschitz Hessians in the vicinity of the solution. On the contrary, our
superlinear convergence results are based on the weaker (γ-order) semismoothness Assumptions 3
and 4 and these assumptions are made only at the solution, rather than in the whole vicinity.
We would like to underline that Lemma 4.3 implies that the results on superlinear convergence
in Theorem 4.2 hold also in the infinite-dimensional C 2 setting. This also means that we have a
hierarchy of results: for f∈C 1,1, we have results on linear and sublinear convergence rates, for
f∈C 2, these results asymptotically improve, and for f∈C 2,2, it is possible to obtain improved
convergence rates. The intermediate case f∈C 2 is not well explored in the literature and our paper
gives some understanding of how algorithms should work in this situation.
To simplify the presentation, we assumed local strong convexity in Assumption 2. This can be relaxed
to local convexity to prove Proposition 4.1 and to F satisfying locally the PL condition, quadratic
growth, andx ∗ being isolated to prove the inequalities in Theorems 4.2 and 4.5.
Our theoretical results are not limited to the PL setting. As stated in Theorems 4.2 and 4.5, transition
to superlinear convergence happens whenever xk →x ∗, which can be established in settings beyond
PL, e.g., under strict convexity. Importantly, all our results are proved for a single class-agnostic
method and there is no need to check in advance whether the PL condition holds or not. And even
in the cases when PL is difficult to verify, we have global rate given by Theorems 3.2 and 3.5. Our
Theorem 3.2 states that the algorithm stays on the level set and that each iteration decreases the value
of the objective. Thus, it is sufficient to assume PL on a level set.
Regarding our results in the finite-dimensional C 2 setting, the reader might wonder why we have
both Theorem 3.5 (in particular, the statement in parentheses) and Theorem 4.9. The reason is that in
the former, the PL inequality is not needed, unlike in Theorem 4.9.
