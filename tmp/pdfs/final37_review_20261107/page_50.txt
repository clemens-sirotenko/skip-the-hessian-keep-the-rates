Fifth, our theoretical results quantify the number of Hessian evaluations for a fixed lazy-update period
m, but they do not provide a principled rule for choosing m in practice. The best value of m depends
on the relative cost of Hessian evaluations, linear-system solves, and backtracking steps, as well as
on the local geometry of the objective. Developing adaptive rules for selecting the Hessian-update
frequency is therefore an important practical question left open by our theory.
Finally, although our experiments cover relevant machine learning problems, they remain relatively
small-scale. In particular, the neural-network example is intended as a first demonstration of semis-
mooth second-order solvers for Lipschitz-constrained training, but a more realistic imaging processing
or inverse problems setup would require parametrizing high-dimensional denoisers. To the best of
our knowledge, this is the first work to rigorously demonstrate semismooth Newton-type solvers with
global convergence rates on such classes of problems. At present, however, direct applicability is
limited to small- and medium-scale settings. Extending the approach to stochastic or randomized
Newton-type methods is an important direction for making it practical in higher dimensions.
We also note that our other assumptions, such as the global minimum assumption, (strong) convex-
ity, Lipschitz derivative, semismoothness are standard for the theoretical analysis of optimization
algorithms.
