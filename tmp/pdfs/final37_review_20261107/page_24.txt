The definition of Newton derivative we use results from taking a selection from the set-valued
map G at each point. Our Newton derivative can be viewed as an abuse of notation where a
particular realization of the derivative has been chosen; in a sense then, our algorithm also
can handle set-valued derivatives.
• A commonly-used definition of semismoothness in literature is the following. The map T
issemismoothat x if it is locally Lipschitz, directionally differentiable at x, and for any

We did not specify which particular maps H(x) are used in our definition of Newton differ-
entiability. We could choose, for example, a single-valued selection of Clarke’s Jacobian
as generalized derivative H(x) and in this way we recover semismoothness (provided the
Lipschitz condition holds). Moreover, if f ′ is semismooth, every single-valued selection of
Clarke’s Jacobian works asHin the above definition.
• In light of these facts, and following established nomenclature within the community, we
use the terms semismoothness and Newton differentiability interchangeably. Particularly,
Newton differentiability implies Assumption 3 and one can choose Newton derivative asH in
our algorithm. At the same time, let us underline that our paper covers a much more general
setting that extends the notion of differentiability even further than semismooth functions.
Indeed, despite beinglessrestrictive than other definitions, Newton differentiability is
sufficient for a local superlinear convergence analysis, as we see in Section 4.
For more details and comparisons, we refer to [65].
A.2 Further discussion of our algorithm
A key feature of our method is that we allow for lazy Hessian updates, with fresh Hessians being
taken only every m iterations. To be precise, we utilize H(x 0) in the trial step (1) for iterates
k= 0, . . . m−1, then H(x m) for iterates k=m, . . . ,2m−1, and so on. This is, as mentioned, the
same setup considered in [19] (but under Lipschitz Hessian assumptions there).
